Free-space material measurement · scalar conversion methods

Choose how complex S-parameters become εr and μr

Three methods are available for a calibrated, homogeneous, isotropic free-space slab. NRW supports normal or oblique incidence with explicit TE or TM polarization. NIST iterative and new non-iterative remain restricted to normal incidence and retrieve permittivity while assuming μr = 1. All need complex reflection and transmission and known normal thickness.

High temperature describes the source's sample category; the app applies no temperature correction. Why SCL is excluded. The source's SCL uses S11 in a short-circuited coaxial or waveguide fixture (p. 15). It cannot be applied unchanged to an unbacked two-horn free-space slab. A metal-backed free-space reflection inversion needs a separate forward model and is outside these three options. Resonator, probe and guided-fixture methods are also outside this free-space selector.

Advantages and disadvantages at a glance

Summarized for the free-space implementation. The source discusses NRW, NIST iterative and the new non-iterative method on pp. 12–14. Benefits remain conditional on the slab and calibration assumptions below.

NRW

εᵣ and μᵣ retrieved

Advantages

  • Fast, direct calculation with no iterative starting guess.
  • Retrieves both scalar permittivity and permeability.
  • Useful when magnetic response cannot be assumed absent.

Disadvantages

  • Poor conditioning near low-loss half-wave resonances.
  • The phase branch still needs physical justification.
  • Weak transmission and small calibration or thickness errors can strongly affect the result.

Free-space specialization of Annex 2. The implementation excludes ill-conditioned results rather than filling gaps by interpolation.

NIST iterative

εᵣ retrieved · μᵣ = 1 assumed

Advantages

  • Fixing μᵣ reduces the unknowns to complex permittivity.
  • Avoids NRW's separate impedance division near half-wave resonances.
  • Can support low-loss and longer slabs with a suitable initial estimate.

Disadvantages

  • Applies only when a nonmagnetic assumption is justified.
  • Requires an initial estimate and iterative convergence.
  • Can reach a different phase branch or fail to converge; residual alone does not prove the result.

Newton solution of the sample-surface, free-space determinant equation from Annex 4. Permeability is fixed, not independently measured.

New non-iterative

εᵣ retrieved · μᵣ = 1 assumed

Advantages

  • Fast, direct propagation-based permittivity calculation.
  • Needs no iterative initial permittivity estimate.
  • Avoids dividing the propagation index by an independently retrieved impedance.

Disadvantages

  • Applies only when a nonmagnetic assumption is justified.
  • Logarithmic phase branches remain ambiguous.
  • Interface extraction can still be ill-conditioned; low transmission and model mismatch remain limits.

Annex 5, equation (3.10), method order one. Improved conditioning described in the source is not a guarantee that every noisy or singular dataset is recoverable.

  • The slab is flat, homogeneous, isotropic, reciprocal and surrounded by air on both sides. Its normal thickness is d, with both reference planes at the slab surfaces.
  • Each input spectrum represents one fixed angle θ relative to the sample normal and one TE or TM polarization. NRW uses the oblique equations below; NIST and new non-iterative require θ = 0. TE and TM are equivalent at normal incidence. Guided modes, anisotropy and cross-polarization require different models.
  • The calibrated channels must represent the slab's reflection and transmission in the same normalized plane-wave mode. The illuminated sample must support the assumed slab/plane-wave approximation. An Rx observation arc is not an incidence-angle sweep.
  • A two-horn arc reflection transfer is commonly raw VNA S21. After calibration it can supply the sample reflection coefficient used as S11 in this inversion. Raw horn-port S11 contains antenna mismatch. Raw arc S21 is not automatically the sample S21 transmission.
  • The importer reads the channel labels in the file. It does not perform calibration, background subtraction, time gating, reference-plane correction or channel reassignment.

Time convention and variables

e+jωt,   k0 = ω/c0,   P = e−jk₀qd,   q = kz/k0,   εr = ε′ − jε″,   μr = μ′ − jμ″

Here j is the imaginary unit; ω = 2πf is angular frequency; c0 is the speed of light; n is the bulk refractive index with n² = εrμr; q is normalized normal propagation; and z is slab wave impedance normalized to air for the selected polarization. At normal incidence q = n. For a passive local isotropic material, ε″ and μ″ are nonnegative and |P| ≤ 1. The Gaussian beam parameter in the geometry guide also uses the traditional letter q; it has units of length and is a different quantity.

NRW: 1. Slab scattering and interface reflection

Denote the calibrated complex sample reflection and transmission by a = S11 and b = S21. The source's transmission coefficient T is called P here to distinguish propagation from transmitted power.

a = Γ(1 − P²)/(1 − Γ²P²),    b = (1 − Γ²)P/(1 − Γ²P²)
X = (1 + a² − b²)/(2a),    Γ = X ± √(X² − 1)

These are the slab equations and source equations (1.1)–(1.2), p. 19. The passive root is selected. Direct division by a becomes unstable near vanishing reflection.

Equivalent impedance expression used by this app

z = √[((1 + a)² − b²)/((1 − a)² − b²)],    Γ = (z − 1)/(z + 1)

The sign of z is chosen with nonnegative real part, as required for passive forward power flow. For a purely imaginary impedance, the tie is resolved using the decaying propagation factor. This equivalent algebra handles a matched lossy slab with S11 = 0 when its impedance expression remains well conditioned. When its numerator or denominator approaches zero, the NRW calculation excludes that frequency as a singularity. Each method also reports its own numerical and reconstruction checks.

2. Propagation through the material

P = (a + b − Γ)/(1 − (a + b)Γ)

This is source equation (1.3), p. 19. The app uses the equivalent expression:

P = b/(1 − aΓ)

Transmission at or below the selected amplitude floor is excluded. The floor must reflect the calibrated instrument's noise and dynamic range; the software default is a computational threshold, not an instrument specification.

3. Normal propagation and material parameters

qm = [−φu + 2πm + j ln|P|]/(k0d),   kz = k0qm
At θ = 0:   εr = qm/z,   μr = qmz

φu is the optionally unwrapped phase of P, and m is the user-selected integer branch. At normal incidence these reduce to source equations (1.4)–(1.6), p. 20. At oblique incidence use the conversion below.

Oblique NRW: polarization-specific conversion

Conservation of tangential wave number gives kx = k0 sin θ and q² = εrμr − sin²θ. The same slab-scattering inverse recovers q and z, but the conversion into constitutive scalars depends on polarization. Set c = cos θ.

Zair,TE = η0/c,   zTE = μrc/q   ⇒   μr = zq/c,   εr = (q² + sin²θ)/μr
Zair,TM = η0c,   zTM = q/(εrc)   ⇒   εr = q/(zc),   μr = (q² + sin²θ)/εr
n = √(εrμr),   sign selected consistently with retrieved q

η0 is the intrinsic free-space impedance. R/T use tangential electric-field amplitudes with identical air modes on both sides. Some total-field TM conventions reverse the reflected coefficient's sign; calibration and inversion must use the same convention. Grazing incidence with cos θ < 10−6 is rejected; singular material conversions remain unavailable. Reconstruction uses a separate oblique forward calculation from the recovered ε and μ.

Comparing TE and TM

Each spectrum has its own fixed angle and normal thickness. Comparison requires exact matching of frequency grid, angle and thickness and opposite polarization; no interpolation is performed. Plots show both estimates, Δε = εTE − εTM, Δμ = μTE − μTM, and real/imaginary input and reconstructed R/T. Agreement is a useful isotropic-model consistency check. Disagreement can reflect calibration, angle, polarization, finite-beam or other model/measurement inconsistency; it does not automatically diagnose anisotropy. Numerical validity is independent of illumination guidance.

NIST iterative: solve for εr with μr = 1

Nonmagnetic model

Let n = √εr on a passive propagation branch. With zero cutoff and reference planes at the sample surfaces, Annex 4's determinant equation reduces to:

Γ = (1 − n)/(1 + n),   P = e−jk₀nd,   Dmeas = S11² − S21²
Dmodel(εr) = (Γ² − P²)/(1 − Γ²P²)

Write εr = ε′ − jε″ and form two real residuals from the complex difference. The Newton update uses a finite-difference Jacobian:

x = [ε′, ε″]T,   F(x) = [Re(Dmodel − Dmeas), Im(Dmodel − Dmeas)]T
Jij = ∂Fi/∂xj,   xnext = x − J−1F(x)

Free-space, sample-surface reduction of source equation (2.8), p. 25; Newton/Jacobian procedure on pp. 25–26. The air-line reference-plane factors in the source are removed by calibration to the sample surfaces. The direct method solves the two-channel determinant. The optional four-channel wrapper first applies the reciprocal, symmetric projection described above, then checks the reconstruction against all four raw channels.

  • The initial ε′ and loss ε″ select the neighborhood explored by the root finder. A nearby converged sweep point can support continuity.
  • A converged determinant root must also reproduce the individual measured S11 and S21 and satisfy the passive model. The determinant alone discards information.
  • Convergence and small residual do not resolve every branch ambiguity or establish that μr = 1 is valid.

New non-iterative: propagation index squared

Nonmagnetic model

Annex 5 introduces effective parameters. Let Λ denote the source's material propagation wavelength parameter and λ0g its empty-cell wavelength. In free space λ0g = λ0. Rename the source's method order n to p here, keeping n for refractive index:

εeff,new = εeff μeffp = [(1 − Γ)/(1 + Γ)]p−1 (λ0g/Λ)p+1
p = 1,   λ0/Λ = nm   ⇒   εr = nm²,   μr = 1 (assumed)

The implementation obtains the propagation eigenvalue directly from the two slab channels using the algebraically equivalent transfer relation, avoiding a direct division by S11:

C = (1 − S11² + S21²)/(2 S21),   P = C ± √(C² − 1)

A passive outgoing root is selected using interface information and continuity where available. The phase logarithm then gives the propagation index:

nm = [−φu + 2πm + j ln|P|]/(k0d)

Source equations (3.1)–(3.10), pp. 27–28, specialized to free space. The value one in source equation (3.10) is the method order, not a declaration that the sample refractive index equals one.

  • Setting μr to one removes its independent retrieval and the final NRW division by impedance.
  • The interface-root and propagation extraction still need valid complex channels. Half-wave conditioning can still affect that extraction even though the final constitutive formula is simplified.
  • For a magnetic sample this assumption can bias εr. Inspect reconstructed S11/S21; the returned μr is an assumption.

The branch must be justified

The measured propagation factor repeats after a whole phase turn. Frequency unwrapping follows continuous changes in a sufficiently dense sweep, but it cannot determine how many complete turns occurred before the first frequency. The selected integer m is added to the unwrapped phase result. The returned per-frequency branch includes both that offset and the turns tracked by unwrapping.

τP = −(1/2π) dφu/df

The source discusses group delay and initial material estimates on pp. 20–21. Independent prior knowledge, additional thickness measurements or a justified dispersion model can support branch selection. A small reconstruction error alone cannot establish the correct branch: different branches can reproduce the same S-parameters. Across excluded data, unwrapping restarts because the missing phase turns are unknown.

What the retrieved scalars mean for tensors

Under the isotropic assumption, NRW returns the two scalars below. For NIST iterative and new non-iterative, the permeability matrix is the identity by assumption; it is not an independent permeability measurement:

ε̿r = εr000εr000εr   μ̿r = μr000μr000μr

A single co-polar S11/S21 pair does not independently determine the entries of a general permittivity or permeability tensor. The symbolic tensor workspace describes the additional angle, azimuth and polarization channels used to formulate that larger inverse problem.

εr,eff(ω) = εr,dielectric(ω) − jσ(ω)/(ωε0)

The retrieved electric loss includes conduction. Separating ε″ from conductivity σ requires further information or a justified broadband model. This retrieval does not generate a separate conductivity tensor.
