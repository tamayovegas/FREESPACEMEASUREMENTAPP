Meaning of an entry

Component coupling

This is a constitutive component map, not a prediction of an S-parameter channel. Measured polarization conversion depends on the complete wave and boundary problem.

D = ε₀ εr E   ·   B = μ₀ μr H   ·   J = σ E
εr,eff(ω) = εr(ω) − j σ(ω)/(ωε₀),   using exp(+jωt)

εr and μr are dimensionless relative tensors; σ has units S/m. D is electric displacement, E electric field, B magnetic flux density, H magnetic field, and J conduction-current density. Bold symbols are matrices/vectors. Superscripts m and L denote coordinate frames. Zeros and identity entries are structural assumptions, not measured values.

One scalar complex S11/S21 pair does not uniquely specify three general tensors. At one frequency, intrinsic electric loss and conductivity also enter through the same effective permittivity. The retrieval and measurement-plan tabs show what additional constraints and observations are needed.

Angles: sample orientation, incidence and polarization are different variables

The slab is parallel-faced and surrounded by air. In general anisotropy, internal eigenmodes have different normal wave numbers, and energy flow need not follow k. The sketch does not assign refractive angles or tensor values.

Where the receiver must look

k̂ᵢ = [cosθ, sinθ, 0]ᵀ in the app's horn-layout plane
k̂ᵣ = k̂ᵢ − 2(k̂ᵢ · n)n,   k̂ₜ = k̂ᵢ in the exit air

k̂Rx points from sample center to Rx. For the specular plane-wave S11 equations, the receiver collects the reflected direction (Δᵣ = 0); for transmission through a parallel slab in air it collects the emerging direction (Δₜ = 0), with possible lateral beam displacement. The app keeps horns fixed when the sample rotates, so a nonzero mismatch is not described by the specular slab S11/S21 alone. Finite-aperture coupling and off-specular scattering require additional modeling.

For the illustrative diagonal material tensor and a rotation γ about z:

T_xxᴸ = T_x cos²γ + T_y sin²γ

Off-diagonal laboratory entries can arise solely from rotating an anisotropic material. An isotropic tensor T I stays unchanged. A general lossy tensor need not be diagonalizable by a real rotation, and ε, μ and σ need not share principal axes.

S-parameters are mode-to-mode coefficients, not material tensor entries

Sample S11 versus instrument S11: the equations here use calibrated, de-embedded sample-face reflection S11 and sample transmission S21. With separate Tx and Rx horns connected to instrument ports, a bistatic reflection path is usually recorded as instrument S21refl. Raw feed-port S11 includes antenna mismatch. The channel mapping, reference planes, background and antenna response must be established before material retrieval.
[b₁; b₂] = S [a₁; a₂],   a₂ = 0 ⇒ b₁ = S₁₁ a₁,   b₂ = S₂₁ a₁
aₚ = [ap,TE, ap,TM]ᵀ,   bₚ = [bp,TE, bp,TM]ᵀ

Reflection polarization block

S₁₁ = S11TE←TES11TE←TMS11TM←TES11TM←TM

Transmission polarization block

S₂₁ = S21TE←TES21TE←TMS21TM←TES21TM←TM

Rows are analyzed outgoing polarization; columns are incident polarization. Diagonal entries are co-polarized channels; off-diagonal entries are cross-polarized channels. Each entry is a complex function of ω, α, φ, Q and thickness d. These two blocks are part of a full two-sided, two-polarization scattering matrix that also contains S12 and S22.

Spqa←b = |Spqa←b| exp(jϕpqa←b)
u(ψ) = [cosψ, sinψ]ᵀ,   v(χ) = [cosχ, sinχ]ᵀ
s₁₁(χ,ψ) = v(χ)† S₁₁ u(ψ),   s₂₁(χ,ψ) = v(χ)† S₂₁ u(ψ)

† is conjugate transpose. Complex Jones vectors extend these expressions to elliptical/circular polarization. A single antenna-polarization setting observes one scalar projection of each block, not the complete blocks. Magnitudes alone discard phase needed for a general retrieval. Reciprocity relates properly normalized reciprocal ports/directions; do not assume arbitrary same-angle cross-polarized entries are equal without fixing those conventions.

R(ψ) = ‖S₁₁ u(ψ)‖²,   T(ψ) = ‖S₂₁ u(ψ)‖²
A(ψ) = 1 − R(ψ) − T(ψ)

These power expressions require consistent power-normalized modes, all outgoing propagating polarization channels, and an ideal slab model without unmeasured edge/radiation channels. Different port impedances or reference-field normalizations require corresponding admittance factors. Absorption alone is not enough to reconstruct tensor components.

Mathematics of angle-dependent measurements

There are three levels: an exact principal-plane diagonal model, its isotropic limit, and the general rotated-tensor boundary problem. Do not insert an arbitrary tensor entry into an isotropic Fresnel equation.

Aligned diagonal tensor · incidence plane xₘ–yₘ

Assume diagonal εr,eff = diag(εₓ, εᵧ, ε_z), μr = diag(μₓ, μᵧ, μ_z), a normal xₘ thickness direction, k_y/k₀ = sinα and k_z = 0. TE has E along zₘ; TM has H along zₘ. Here εᵢ already includes dielectric and conduction loss.

q_TE²(α) = ε_z μ_y − (μ_y/μ_x) sin²α
q_TM²(α) = μ_z ε_y − (ε_y/ε_x) sin²α

Y₀,TE = cosα                 Y₁,TE = q_TE / μ_y
Y₀,TM = 1/cosα               Y₁,TM = ε_y / q_TM

Γ_p(α) = (Y₀,p − Y₁,p) / (Y₀,p + Y₁,p)
P_p(α) = exp[−j k₀ q_p(α) d]                 p ∈ {TE, TM}

S₁₁ᵖ(α) = Γ_p(1 − P_p²) / (1 − Γ_p² P_p²)
S₂₁ᵖ(α) = (1 − Γ_p²) P_p / (1 − Γ_p² P_p²)

q is the normal wave number normalized by k₀ = ω/c₀. Y is tangential wave admittance normalized to air's intrinsic admittance. Select passive outgoing/decaying modal branches. The slab has air on both sides and known thickness d, and the coefficients use a consistent tangential-electric-field convention. Internal multiple reflections are included. Cross-polarized terms vanish only under these aligned principal-plane assumptions.

Angle sensitivity is explicit: TE contains μₓ through its sin²α term; TM contains εₓ. At normal incidence these terms disappear. Thus normal-incidence measurements alone do not probe the normal tensor components in this aligned model.

Isotropic limit

εₓ = εᵧ = ε_z = ε,   μₓ = μᵧ = μ_z = μ
q²(α) = ε μ − sin²α,   Y₁,TE = q/μ,   Y₁,TM = ε/q

This is the forward-model family used by the app's existing numerical material tab. The symbolic tensor workspace does not convert that solver into a general anisotropic solver.

General tensors and arbitrary azimuth / sample rotation

k × [μr−1(k × E)] + k₀² εr,eff E = 0
k∥,in = k∥,slab = k∥,out
Ψ = [E_y, E_z, Z₀H_y, Z₀H_z]ᵀ,   ∂Ψ/∂x = −j k₀ B(ε, μ, k_y/k₀, k_z/k₀) Ψ
Ψ(d) = exp(−j k₀ d B) Ψ(0)

Here the field-state coordinates are the slab frame, x is normal, Z₀ = √(μ₀/ε₀) is the air impedance used to normalize H, and B is a tangential-field propagation operator obtained from Maxwell's equations—not magnetic flux density B from the constitutive law. Tangential E/H continuity at both faces converts the internal modal solution into the external S blocks. Modal scattering algorithms are preferable to direct growing transfer matrices in strongly lossy or evanescent cases. This formulation excludes magnetoelectric coupling; bianisotropic media require additional ξ/ζ constitutive tensors.

The tensors are inferred jointly from measurements

calibrated complex S₁₁, S₂₁→angles + polarizations + d→tensor forward model→constrained inverse fit→ε̂, μ̂, σ̂ + uncertainty
ϑ = {independent tensor coefficients and any chosen dispersion parameters}
yℓ = vec{S₁₁(ωℓ,αℓ,φℓ,Qℓ), S₂₁(ωℓ,αℓ,φℓ,Qℓ)}
ϑ̂ = arg minϑ∈C Σℓ ‖Wℓ1/2 [Fℓ(ϑ;d) − yℓ]‖² + λreg ℛ(ϑ)

ϑ is the material parameter vector; θ remains the horn-layout angle. F solves the anisotropic forward problem. vec stacks the measured complex channels; unavailable channels are omitted. W represents measurement weighting, C the physical constraints, ℛ optional regularization, and λreg its weight (not wavelength). A real residual stacks real and imaginary parts. No fit is run in this symbolic workspace.

Constraint / diagnosticMathematics and purpose
Reciprocity, when justifiedεᵀ = ε, μᵀ = μ and symmetric conductivity under the stated non-bianisotropic reciprocal model. Complex symmetric is not the same as Hermitian.
Passivity, exp(+jωt)−(ε_eff − ε_eff†)/(2j) ⪰ 0 and −(μ − μ†)/(2j) ⪰ 0. For real conductivity modeled separately, its symmetric part must be positive semidefinite.
IdentifiabilityForm the real sensitivity Jacobian J = ∂[Re F; Im F]/∂ϑ_real. Rank deficiency or very small singular values indicates unobservable or poorly separated parameter combinations. More data do not guarantee uniqueness.
UncertaintyUse measurement noise and calibration/geometry uncertainty. A local linear estimate is Cov(ϑ̂) ≈ (Jᵀ W_real J)⁻¹ only when this information matrix is nonsingular and the noise model/linearization apply.

Only ε_eff = ε_r − jσ/(ωε₀) enters the usual frequency-domain electric constitutive response. A single frequency cannot separate arbitrary intrinsic dielectric loss from conductivity. Even a broadband fit needs a justified dispersion/conduction model or independent conductivity information. Unknown thickness, reference-plane offsets, incomplete polarization channels and branch ambiguity can also make the inverse problem nonunique.

Scalar normal-incidence S11/S21 retrieval — useful special case, not a tensor inverse

The numerical retrieval tab also supports oblique TE/TM NRW; see its method guide for the polarization-specific conversion. The equations here retain the normal-incidence special case for an isotropic homogeneous symmetric slab of known thickness in air, with calibrated complex sample-face coefficients s₁₁ and s₂₁:

X = (1 + s₁₁² − s₂₁²) / (2 s₁₁)
Γ = X ± sqrt(X² − 1)
P = (s₁₁ + s₂₁ − Γ) / [1 − Γ(s₁₁ + s₂₁)]

z = (1 + Γ)/(1 − Γ)                 [impedance / air impedance]
n_m = [j Log(P) + 2πm] / (k₀ d)    [integer branch m]
ε_eff = n_m / z                    μ_r = n_m z

Passive behavior, frequency continuity and independent constraints select consistent signs/branches; they do not always resolve ambiguity. Reflection zeros and slab resonances make these algebraic formulas singular or ill-conditioned. These equations retrieve scalar effective parameters only. In the tensor case, use the multi-angle/polarization forward fit above.

Acquisition geometry

How the acquisition count is formed

Ng = Nα Nφ NQ
Ntraces = Ng Npol Nblocks
Ncomplex = Ntraces Nf

With one angle or azimuth setting, the start value is used and the stop value is ignored. These are scheduled acquisitions, including any repeated angle settings. Orientations must correspond to separately documented, known rotations Q; entering a count does not define those rotations. Counts do not establish the rank of the inverse problem. The same measured channels constrain permittivity and permeability jointly, so their acquisition counts are not added twice. A complete plan also records angle uncertainty, polarization basis, normal thickness, calibration and reference planes.

Which measurements constrain the tensors?

Measurement familyWhat it probes / why it is needed
Complex co-polarized TE and TM S11/S21Separates the principal-plane TE and TM wave responses. Retain amplitude and phase with documented power/field normalization.
Incidence αₐ, αᵦ, …Changes the sin²α terms; provides sensitivity to normal components εₓ and μₓ in the aligned diagonal case. Use several distinct, well-characterized angles, not just repeated settings.
Azimuth φₐ, φᵦ, …Changes the tangential direction relative to material axes, separating in-plane anisotropy.
Cross-polarized channels TE←TM and TM←TEObserves polarization conversion. A single co-polarized pair can hide off-diagonal effects and parameter degeneracies.
Sample rotations Qₐ, Qᵦ, …Provides independent projections of the same material tensors. Rotations, surface-normal orientation, and thickness must be known.
Frequency ωₐ, ωᵦ, …Helps track propagation branches and fit a specified dispersion model; arbitrary frequency dependence still cannot independently separate all loss mechanisms.
Reverse-side / additional port channels, when neededS22 and S12 can add constraints and test symmetry/reciprocity assumptions. They are not silently inferred from incomplete data.
Calibration and independent informationEstablish sample reference planes, empty-path/background correction, polarization basis, thickness d, angle uncertainty, gain/phase drift and fixture response. Independent nonmagnetic or conductivity knowledge reduces unknowns only if physically justified.
There is no universal number of angles that guarantees recovery of a full tensor. Choose a material class, count independent parameters, examine the sensitivity rank, and validate on measurements excluded from fitting.

Notation dictionary

ε and μ: relative material tensors; σ: electrical conductivity tensor, not RCS. Q: real sample-to-laboratory rotation. m/L: material/laboratory frame. xₘ: thickness normal; yₘ,zₘ: sample plane. α: incidence; φ: incidence azimuth; ψ/χ: source/analyzer polarization; ϕ: complex S-parameter phase. d: normal thickness; ω: angular frequency; k₀: air wave number; q: normalized normal modal wave number; Γ: one-interface amplitude reflection; P: one-pass propagation. Hat (ˆ): inferred estimate. All tensor symbols remain unassigned.
