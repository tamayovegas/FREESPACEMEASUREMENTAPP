Variables, readouts and equations

For symbolic permittivity, permeability and conductivity tensors, their rotation, polarized S11/S21 blocks, angle-dependent equations and retrieval assumptions, open the app's Symbolic tensors & S-parameters workspace or the standalone symbolic guide. Tensor entries remain letters; the numerical simulator below remains isotropic.

This app combines a geometric/Gaussian illumination visualization with a passive isotropic slab simulation. Material properties determine reflected, transmitted and absorbed power. Presets are illustrative, not measured data. SI units are used in equations unless marked otherwise.

When the aperture is unknown, the app assumes a square opening with area Aap = D²/2 and aperture efficiency ηap = 0.50. The nominal gain gdBi is referenced to an isotropic antenna. The gain-to-aperture relation gives:

G = 10^(g_dBi / 10)                    λ_center = c₀ / f_center
G = 4π η_ap A_ap / λ_center²          D = λ_center sqrt(G / (2π η_ap))

The estimated D stays fixed across the low-to-high frequency sweep and is recomputed if the center frequency or nominal gain changes. This is a generic geometry estimate. It does not identify a particular calibrated horn, validate its gain pattern, or guarantee operation across the band. For real measurements, enter the measured or manufacturer-specified maximum opening dimension in Known aperture (manual).

The region formulas are approximate bare-horn aperture criteria, even with lenses enabled. A lens does not change this reference R_FF; the horn-plus-lens system requires its own illumination and wavefront analysis. No reactive electromagnetic fields are solved.

The field-region plot evaluates these formulas at 240 uniformly spaced frequencies from the selected low endpoint to the high endpoint, inclusive. Geometry, footprints, the beam-radius plot and single-frequency readouts use f = fcenter.

3. Gaussian beam and power interception

w₀ = 0.425 D                          z_R = π w₀² / λ
w(s) = w₀ sqrt(1 + (s/z_R)²)           [without lens]
I(r_perp,s) / I(0,s) = exp(−2 r_perp² / w(s)²)
I(0,s) = 2 P_beam / (π w(s)²)          [if total beam power P_beam is known]

q_in = ℓ + i z_R                      q_after = 1 / (1/q_in − 1/F)
s_focus = ℓ − Re(q_after)              z_R,out = Im(q_after)
w_min = sqrt(λ z_R,out / π)
w(s) = w_min sqrt(1 + ((s−s_focus)/z_R,out)²), for s > ℓ

B = [u_hatᵀ; v_hatᵀ]                  M = B (I₃ − kᵢ kᵢᵀ) Bᵀ
r_perp² = [u v] M [u v]ᵀ
η = ∬_sample {2 |kᵢ·n| / (π w²)} exp(−2 [u v] M [u v]ᵀ / w²) du dv
Intercepted percentage = 100 η

At zero sample rotation:
η = erf(W cos(θ)/(sqrt(2) w)) × erf(H/(sqrt(2) w))

w₀ is the assumed waist at the horn aperture, not a measured pattern. z_R is the Rayleigh range. s is distance along the Tx beam. q is the complex Gaussian beam parameter; i = √−1, Re and Im mean real and imaginary parts. P_beam is total beam power, not an input used for these normalized images. r_perp is distance perpendicular to the beam axis. u and v (m) are sample-local coordinates, u_hat and v_hat are their unit vectors, B maps sample coordinates into space, M is the two-dimensional projection metric, I₃ is the identity matrix, and erf is the Gaussian error function. The integral is over −W/2 ≤ u ≤ W/2 and −H/2 ≤ v ≤ H/2.

The footprint freezes w at the plane center, neglecting changes across a tilted sample. At R = 0 it illustrates the Gaussian launch plane with w(0) = w₀; an ideal lens is inactive when ℓ ≥ R. This limiting geometry does not model electromagnetic contact coupling. Estimates are suppressed for |kᵢ·n| < 0.05 and flagged at incidence above 70°. Aperture clipping, lens aberrations, diffraction, anisotropic response and finite-sample polarization coupling are not calculated by the Gaussian footprint. The separate isotropic slab calculation is described below.

Gaussian illumination diagnostics

q_beam(sample) = R + i z_R                         [no active lens]
q_beam(sample) = q_after + R - ℓ                   [active lens before sample]
1 / R_wave = Re(1 / q_beam(sample))
θ_div ≈ λ / (π w_active)                          [radians; half-angle]

ρ²(u,v) = [u v] M [u v]ᵀ
Δφ_max = (k₀ / 2) |1/R_wave| max_sample ρ²         [radians]
E_boundary,max / E_axis = exp(−min_boundary ρ² / w_sample²)
Boundary field (dB) = −20 min_boundary ρ² / (ln(10) w_sample²)

Exact spherical reference: Δφ_sphere = (2π/λ)(sqrt(R_wave² + a²) − |R_wave|)

R_wave is the signed local wavefront curvature radius; it is infinite at a Gaussian waist, so curvature phase approaches zero. w_active is the launch waist without a lens, or the outgoing lens-transformed waist. θ_div is the paraxial divergence half-angle, not an antenna half-power beamwidth. Large divergence also limits the single-angle paraxial approximation. The traditional length-valued Gaussian q_beam differs from the dimensionless normal propagation q = k_z/k₀ used in retrieval.

The quadratic phase is measured relative to the center tangent plane, excluding the linear incidence phase. For the rotated rectangle, maximum ρ² occurs at a corner. The boundary-field calculation minimizes the projected quadratic metric along each of the four finite edges, including interior edge minima; it therefore reports the strongest boundary field. Field dB uses 20 log₁₀(E/E_axis), not 10 log₁₀ of field. These estimates freeze the beam width and curvature at the sample center, consistent with the existing footprint approximation. They are unavailable near grazing. At R = 0 they describe an idealized launch plane.

Engineering guidance: phase variation ≤ 22.5° and boundary field ≤ −20 dB can guide a setup review. They are not accuracy guarantees or standards-compliance tests. Bare-horn R_FF = 2D²/λ does not guarantee a flat wavefront over an arbitrarily large sample. Illumination warnings remain separate from numerical retrieval validity, and do not correct measured S-parameters.

The exact spherical formula is exported as a separate geometric reference, not substituted for Gaussian quadratic phase. At 10 GHz, |R_wave| = 1 m and a = 100 mm it gives approximately 59.9°; the quadratic approximation gives approximately 60.0°. All diagnostic values, their assumptions and the setup snapshot are included in plot bundles. Receiver offset δ retains the reflection/transmission geometry definition below.

See the supplied oblique-incidence note, pp. 8-9, and Fenner and Rothwell's curved-wavefront study. That study concerns a specific conductor-backed reflection-only method; effects depend on the measurement model and material.

Here Rx(a) denotes an x-axis rotation matrix, while “Rx horn” denotes the receiver. kᵢ, k_Rx, k_spec, k_path, n and E_hat are unit vectors. The actual E arrow is drawn at an arbitrary scale. At normal incidence e_TE = v_hat. Transmission geometry draws a straight external path. The slab response includes refraction through its normal wave number and multiple reflections, but the emerging beam's lateral displacement is not drawn.

5. Material presets and constitutive properties

The former measured-data workflow has been replaced by the Material tab. It automatically simulates the same sample in both reflection and transmission geometries. The model is a homogeneous, isotropic, passive slab between two air half-spaces, with coherent internal reflections. It does not acquire measurements.

Perfect conductor (PEC)Opaque ideal boundary: reflected power 100%, transmitted power 0%, absorbed power 0%. Constitutive and thickness controls are disabled because this is an ideal boundary, not a large finite conductivity.
Metal (finite conductivity)Illustrative finite conductor: ε′ = 1, μ′ = 1, σ = 5.8×10⁷ S/m, d = 1 mm. Finite conductivity allows small absorption and, for sufficiently thin slabs, transmission. This is not a validated metal-dispersion dataset.

ω = 2πf                   ε₀ = 8.8541878128 × 10⁻¹² F/m

ε and μ are absolute permittivity (F/m) and permeability (H/m), and μ₀ is vacuum permeability (approximately 4π×10⁻⁷ H/m). The solver uses dimensionless admittances and relative properties. If supplied dielectric loss already includes conduction, set σ = 0 to avoid counting that loss twice. The frequency plot holds ε′, ε″, μ′, μ″ and σ fixed; only the conduction contribution to ε_r,eff varies as 1/f. This is an illustrative limited-band model, not a causal fitted material-dispersion model. Negative-index, anisotropic and active/gain media are outside its scope.

6. Reflection, transmission and absorption equations

k₀ = 2π/λ                        c = cos(α)
q = sqrt(ε_r,eff μ_r − sin²α)      δ = k₀ q d
Choose passive branch Im(q) ≤ 0; if real, choose q ≥ 0.
p = exp(−jδ)                     [single-pass propagation]

Normalized tangential wave admittances:
TE: Y₀ = c,      Y₁ = q/μ_r
TM: Y₀ = 1/c,    Y₁ = ε_r,eff/q

For either polarization:
r₀₁ = (Y₀ − Y₁)/(Y₀ + Y₁)
r = r₀₁ (1 − p²)/(1 − r₀₁² p²)
t = (1 − r₀₁²) p/(1 − r₀₁² p²)
R = |r|²                         T = |t|²
A = 1 − R − T

Y₀ and Y₁ are wave admittances normalized to free-space admittance, outside and inside the slab. The two exterior media are identical, so no extra transmitted-power admittance factor is required. q is normalized normal wave number; δ is normal phase thickness in radians; d must be in meters in these equations. The implementation evaluates equivalent scaled formulas to avoid exponential overflow for thick conductors and uses the analytic limit at q = 0. Exactly grazing incidence is marked unavailable because normal incident power vanishes.

The field coefficients r and t refer to tangential electric fields. The TM reflected-amplitude sign may therefore differ from textbook conventions using a reflected TM basis pointing in the opposite direction. Powers are independent of that convention. Transmission phase is measured between the two slab faces; it includes propagation and is not divided by an empty-air reference. Phase is shown as NaN when a coefficient's magnitude is negligible.

Polarization weights: w_TE = cos²ψ, w_TM = sin²ψ
R_mix = w_TE R_TE + w_TM R_TM
T_mix = w_TE T_TE + w_TM T_TM
A_mix = w_TE A_TE + w_TM A_TM
R_mix + T_mix + A_mix = 1

Transmission insertion loss IL = −10 log10(T_mix) dB
Reflection return loss RL = −10 log10(R_mix) dB
Phase of a field coefficient = arg(r or t) × 180/π degrees

TE and TM are orthogonal, so their powers add with the stated weights. There is no single mixed-polarization phase in the readout; both phases are printed separately. R = 0 gives infinite return loss and T = 0 gives infinite insertion loss. These are mathematical limits, not numerical failures. A high return loss means little reflection; a high insertion loss can result from reflection, absorption, or both.

Material R/T/A frequency plotSimulated fractions at 180 uniformly spaced frequencies from low to high, including both endpoints. Incidence, thickness, polarization and constitutive inputs stay fixed. The dotted line marks the selected center frequency.

Neither R/T/A nor the whole-beam budget is received horn power. Rx alignment, its aperture/mode overlap, finite sample diffraction, edge effects, surface roughness and coherent bypass around the sample are not solved. The slab calculation uses a local plane wave at the center incidence angle; focused beams can contain a spread of angles. A tilted parallel slab produces a parallel emerging beam with lateral displacement, which is not drawn. The geometry arrows show directions with material fractions as labels, not field amplitudes. The drawn fiber stripes remain geometric decoration: the material model is isotropic.

7. Approximate specular flat-plate RCS scale

Reflection mode retains an explicitly approximate specular RCS scale, derived from a uniformly illuminated large rectangular plate and scaled by the modeled reflectance:

Area = W H
σ_RCS,spec ≈ (4π Area² / λ²) cos²α R_mix
σ_RCS,spec[dBsm] = 10 log10(σ_RCS,spec / 1 m²)

This is a scalar Fresnel-scaled plate approximation, not measured RCS or a general full-wave bistatic prediction. It assumes a large, flat sample, uniform plane-wave illumination and far-field observation in the specular direction. The app prints the angular offset to the fixed Rx; when Rx is not in the specular direction, the displayed value is not RCS toward Rx. Near-field/lens configurations are flagged. Arbitrary-angle scattering, diffraction, vector polarization factors and receiver coupling need a more complete solver. Transmission mode does not infer forward-scatter RCS from T.

8. Using the material model

  1. Select Reflection or Transmission and set the low, center and high frequencies in Geometry, along with horn geometry, sample dimensions, rotation and E polarization.
  2. Open Material. Choose PEC, finite-conductivity metal, an example absorber, lossless dielectric, air, or Custom.
  3. Edit ε′, ε″, μ′, μ″, conductivity and thickness as appropriate. The Material response tab updates automatically. To edit a PEC-like material, select finite metal or Custom.
  4. Choose Material R / T / A vs frequency in the fourth plot to compare the power fractions across the selected band. The footprint and beam-radius views remain available.
  5. Use Export simulated result (CSV) to save geometry, frequency settings, material properties, mixed/TE/TM power fractions, phases and model notes for the center frequency. The plot export control saves the graph PNG, a same-stem parameter CSV and a wrapped parameter-table PNG together. The CSV includes all three frequencies in Hz, the other controls, derived values, units, definitions and camera view at export.

9. Angle acquisition planner and scalar S-parameter inverse

The Symbolic tensors & S-parameters workspace receives live geometry, incidence, rotation, frequency and thickness from this setup. Use its setup-copy button to seed one setting, then specify the angular/azimuth sweeps, known orientations, polarization channels and frequency points. The planner distinguishes incidence sweeps from receiver observation arcs and counts geometry settings, traces and complex frequency samples. Matrices remain symbolic; acquisition counts alone do not establish a unique tensor inverse.

N_geometry = N_angle N_azimuth N_orientation
N_trace = N_geometry N_polarization N_blocks
N_complex = N_trace N_frequency

The S-parameters to epsilon / mu tab numerically retrieves scalar isotropic properties using calibrated complex S11/S21 and known normal thickness. NRW supports normal or oblique TE/TM incidence; NIST and New non-iterative remain normal-only. See the PDF method guide for the free-space NRW, NIST iterative and New non-iterative equations, comparison tables, advantage/disadvantage bullets, phase branches and invalidity checks. NIST and New non-iterative fix mu_r = 1 as an explicit nonmagnetic assumption; NRW retrieves both scalars. The input can be a pair or imported four-channel data projected onto a reciprocal symmetric slab and checked against every raw channel. This calculation does not infer arbitrary-angle tensor components. Exported retrieval data preserve the signed imaginary values; graphs also display positive loss as minus the imaginary part.
